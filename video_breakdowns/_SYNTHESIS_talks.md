## 2. Hook archetypes and whether speed correlates with views

Primary archetype counts (n=18): audience-qualifier/conditional 5 (BmQT, GSkY, DYET, CtoP, 6V4y); story cold-open or question 3 (4hX8, BYrk, F8R6); contrarian/negation claim 2 (7ITff, 9p64); enumeration 2 (1RUdv, IFVT); credential flash 2 (3O40, HwWX); one each of quote->rule (0UbO), voiced objection (2OIT), stakes (-86U), and none (4pnc). The "if you [situation]" opener is the most common (5 of 18), and 3 of the top-8 by views use enumeration or negation.

**Do fast openers go with more views here? No evidence of it.** Sample: n=17 with views (CtoP has none). Spearman rank correlation of views vs time-to-first-claim is +0.07; vs words-in-first-3s is -0.39 (n=16 without 4pnc: +0.03 and -0.38). Roughly zero for speed and mildly negative for word density, on 17 points. Seven videos had a first claim later than 6s with a median of 421K views against 61K for the ten at 6s or sooner, but those are dominated by channel size, age and format (2021-2022 uploads from large channels vs reposted clips). Within one creator (Codie Sanchez, own channel, n=3) the fastest opener (DYET, 3.2s) had the fewest views (9.4K vs 57K and 65K). This sample cannot show that hook speed causes views, and it does not show that slow starts are fine either. What it does show is that large videos use many different openers.

## 3. Measured pacing norms

- Words per second (caption words / caption span): mean 3.45, median 3.54, range 1.63-4.27 (n=18). Without 4pnc (music-heavy vlog): mean 3.56. Shorts-length videos (n=9): mean 3.66; the rest (n=9): mean 3.25.
- Words spoken in the first 3s: median 12, mean 11.7, range 7-18 (n=17 with speech). Densest: BmQT, 18 (qualifier plus credential); lightest: 0UbO, 7 (quote).
- Opening sentence length: median ~3.8s, range ~0.8-7.0s (n=17; 4pnc excluded). Most land between 2.5 and 6s. The longest (6V4y, 7.0s; HwWX, 6.7s; 2OIT, ~6s) are dedications or credential statements.
- Time to first substantive claim: 10 of 17 are 6s or sooner; the slowest are the structured long-forms (IFVT ~25s, -86U ~21s) and the sponsor-led 4pnc (~102s).
- Re-hooks inside short videos recur every 5-12s: numbered signposts ("number one/two/three", 1RUdv at 5/12/20s), "here's the thing about" (BmQT, 31s), "Can I just say something to everybody?" (GSkY, 5s).

## 4. Endings and CTAs

- No CTA at all: 9 of 18 (7ITff, 1RUdv, 3O40, 4hX8, 0UbO, BYrk, GSkY, -86U, 9p64). All end on the last line of the argument.
- Explicit platform or audience ask: 7 of 18. Follow only: BmQT ("i'm a psychologist follow for more"). Comment: IFVT, 4pnc, HwWX (twice, at ~7:10 and ~7:55). Like + subscribe + comment: F8R6 (and an earlier "please subscribe" at ~1:00). Like + subscribe + next video: 6V4y. A real-world challenge ("Try it and report back"): 2OIT.
- Product plug: DYET ("at Contrarian Thinking, we've seen most of our people double or triple their sales"). Truncated before any ending: CtoP.
- By length: of the 10 videos at or under ~68s, only 2 carry any ask (BmQT follow, 2OIT challenge). Of the 8 videos over 140s, 5 carry a platform CTA. Shorts-length creators close on the idea; long-form creators close on the ask.
- Ending device used most: restating the thesis in the last sentence ("I'm just faster", "as long as it takes", "you can do it, too").

## 5. Which structures survive the no-editing constraint

Survive fully in one unedited take (12 of 18): 1RUdv, BmQT, 4hX8, 0UbO, BYrk, GSkY, DYET, 9p64, 2OIT, CtoP (the words), 7ITff, HwWX (the words). Partial (2): 3O40 and -86U, each with one segment that is a spoken story. Not feasible (4): F8R6 (multi-guest interview with a cold-open built from later clips), IFVT (on-screen AI demos plus a sponsor read), 6V4y (clip montage with other speakers), 4pnc (music-led multi-clip vlog).

The structures that work in one take, ranked by how little they demand:

1. **Counted list with a withheld hint** (1RUdv): "here's three reasons you X and here's a hint, it's not Y". 27s, signposted by number.
2. **"Someone asked me" + count + a last item that turns on the viewer** (4hX8). Opinion, not credentials.
3. **Quote -> "my one rule" -> reason** (0UbO). 16s.
4. **Difficulty reframe** (9p64): "the harder it is, the fewer people follow". Fits tricking without business authority.
5. **Qualifier -> credential -> single metaphor -> follow** (BmQT). The credential he can use is "coached 100+ students".
6. **Name-drop anecdote -> answer -> named rule** (BYrk). Name the rule earlier than 29s.
7. **Qualifier + reversal, with a mid-clip direct-address re-hook** (GSkY).
8. **A challenge as the ending** (2OIT): "Try it and report back" creates a comment reason without a "comment below".
9. **Negate a belief, then use your own timeline as the proof** (7ITff): fits a 100-follower start.

Structures to avoid under the constraint (all in the words): cold-open teasers assembled from later material (F8R6; the Hormozi opener repeats verbatim at ~0:40, [inference] that it was spliced), played-back footage (3O40, ~90s), screen demos (IFVT), quoted-clip montages (6V4y), and sponsor reads (IFVT, 4pnc).

### The authority problem at 19 (observed in the data)
- Patterns that **borrow** authority and so help: quote first (0UbO), "I asked [named person]" (BYrk), famous-founder stories (IFVT: his own credential arrives at 10:30), "someone asked me" (4hX8).
- Patterns that **declare** authority and so hurt: "if you want to make more money in business" (DYET) and winner/loser pronouncements. radeo (HwWX, 19) pre-empts doubt ("I'm not saying that to brag", "I don't have a rich dad") yet admits "I can't tell you the name of my exact pages." Taita's verifiable assets (Berkeley CS, 8th nationally in 2025, 100+ students coached, bilingual) make lived-experience topics safer than business advice.
- The only close analog (4pnc: engineering plus elite athlete) shows no advice at all; it documents a day. That avoids the authority problem but its speech contains no hook, so the title must do the work.
