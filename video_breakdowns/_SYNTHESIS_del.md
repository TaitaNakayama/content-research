## 3. Patterns that repeat

**Hook archetypes (18).** Instruction/promise: 3 (6yv, Qr1n, 3B). Open question: 3 (MII, -I3, LHF), plus 3Cz's in-media-res question. Contrarian: 1 (Rtb). Stakes: 1 (9tv). Credential flash: 1, delayed (Exg). In-media-res: 2 (3Cz, CPu). None-verbal for at least the first 3s: 4 (GcKk, 7zi, AVM, UFY). Greeting/announcement with no archetype: 3 (95CE, Nlv, EE5 title echo). The dominant verbal pattern is "address the viewer's situation", not "show skill".

**Time to first claim.** Among the 12 videos that open with speech and a claim, 8 reach it inside 3s (Rtb, Qr1n, 3Cz, 9tv, MII, LHF, Nlv topic, CPu location). The creator-introduction preamble ("hey welcome back", "Lucas here", "my name is...") delays the claim in Exg, 6yv, -I3, 3B, CPu by roughly 2-10s. The 4 non-verbal openers are not delayed claims; they are silence (AVM 18s, UFY 12s, GcKk effectively the whole body, 7zi first words 7s).

**Where payoffs land.** In the 7 videos with a single identifiable outcome, 5 land in the last quarter: 6yv (89%), AVM (95%), 9tv (96%), Rtb (~89% demo cue), 3Cz (74%). The exceptions are early: Nlv (43%), Exg (38%). Nothing in the Shorts is withheld longer than their runtime, which is expected. Three of the long-form winners are failure or incomplete outcomes (AVM, 9tv) with the outcome told in the title or first minute, so tension runs on process.

**Tutorial sequencing (6yv, 95CE, EE5, Rtb, MII).** Always: promise or reassurance first; obstacle or reframe second (fear in 95CE at 0:04, "not easy but start today" in EE5, "a cork is basically a crappy cheat gainer with a 360 twist" in 6yv, "almost everybody in the world can do this" in Rtb); easiest move first (EE5 teaches a move by 0:22, Rtb step 1 at 0:05); a milestone line ("now you have your first tricking combo", EE5 6:00); a "key point" flag (6yv 2:03); and a closing bridge to progression (EE5 8:36, MII 8:00). The pacing device in 6yv is a spoken clock.

**Mid-video re-hooks.** Present in long-form only, and usually a plan change or a tease: AVM 7:45 ("I guess I got to place 40"), 9tv 2:52 ("Instead of telling you what this good idea is, I might as well just show it to you"), EE5 3:15 ("Stick around to the end"), LHF 1:00 ("something very interesting"). Rtb repeats its hook phrase at 0:27 and 0:42; Nlv's re-hook is a second question at 0:11.

**Endings.** Generic like/subscribe/comment: 8 of 18. A product or paid offer: 3 (EE5 app with 7-day trial, LHF membership, 3B free call). A coaching-style DM funnel: 1 (MII: "send it to me on Instagram", with a clip). A verification comment: 1 (Qr1n). A series-based follow: 2 (Nlv, 6yv "daily videos Monday through Friday"). No CTA at all: 4 (Rtb, 3Cz, 7zi, UFY), 3 of them Shorts.

## 4. What this corpus tells us about a tricking Short

Supported by the five Shorts, with small n:
1. **A spoken contrarian first sentence works as a hook for a skill Short** (Rtb: "If you can't do a backflip, there's a way to trick people into thinking that you can", 1.1M views). One example; the claim is about hook wording only.
2. **A Short can make the viewer's own answer the loop** (Qr1n): the CTA at 0:25 asks them to confirm the result in the comments. This is the only participation mechanism in the corpus.
3. **Shorts here carry almost no conversion mechanism.** 3 of 5 have no CTA. The one with a follow ask (Nlv) ties it to a series and has 1k views. Nothing in the corpus shows a Short converting views to followers.
4. **A how-to Short can have no spoken instruction at all** (7zi). For that video, the structure is invisible to us.

Not supported: any claim about visuals, cover frames, on-screen text, cuts, trick-reveal timing, or the Kobe-crash-style "watch the landing" hook. If a tricking Short lives on its visual first frame, this corpus is blind to it.

## 5. What this corpus tells us about long-form (kept separate)

- **Tricking tutorial channels end by selling a progression product** (EE5 Trickvault, with a free trial). That is the only tricking-native monetization pattern here, and it fits Taita's CS background.
- **The comeback/landing story in long-form** (Exg) is told chronologically, gives away the result at 1:07, includes an injury, and is plugged with a sponsor at 6:04. The structure leaves open the "crash first, landing held back" ordering, which is Taita's actual arc.
- **Coaching conversion in speech:** MII asks for a clip by DM; 3B states who is not eligible; LHF names the membership at 0:50. These are CTAs that screen or start a one-to-one relationship.
- **Social-growth videos (-I3, LHF, Nlv, 3B) give generic or unverified advice** and, in the transcripts, do not separate views from followers. -I3 never mentions conversion or the profile. LHF's only conversion-adjacent heuristic is that saves and shares indicate content worth revisiting.
