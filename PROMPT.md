# Content Strategy Research: three brands

You are leading an exhaustive research project on short-form content strategy for one creator, Taita Nakayama. Work autonomously; do not stop to ask questions. Where something is ambiguous, pick the most reasonable reading, note it in `ASSUMPTIONS.md`, and keep going.

## Who this is for

Taita is a 19-year-old UC Berkeley CS student, startup builder and competitive tricker (tricking = acrobatic martial arts: flips, twists, kicks). He speaks English and Japanese. He is running three brands:

1. **taita.del: tricking account.** Competitive since 2020, ranked 8th nationally in 2025, coached 100+ students. Several viral videos (a double-backflip crash at a famous gym in Kobe went 10M+, and landing it years later went millions), 10M+ total views but only ~6,000 followers. **The poor views-to-followers conversion is a core problem to solve.**
2. **taita.talks: personal/daily-life talking-head account.** About 100 followers. Topics: daily life, building startups, mindset, what he's learning, possibly relationships or general life takes. He has committed to **raw, one-take talking videos** because filming and editing friction is his real blocker, so recommendations must be low-production.
3. **ごはんとも (Gohantomo): Japanese AI calorie-tracking app (Cal AI-style), mascot もぐもぐ.** The brand account doesn't exist yet. Audience is Japanese. He plans TikTok photo slideshows (a format proven for US apps like Cal AI) and wants to know whether that format works in Japan.

His goal across all three is **audience he can monetize** (products, apps, coaching), not raw views. Platforms: TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts (and Japanese TikTok/Instagram for Gohantomo).

## Research questions

Answer all of these, per brand where relevant, with concrete examples (creator, video, link, view count where findable).

### A. Retention mechanics
- How top creators open and close loops: hooks in the first 1–3 seconds, mid-video re-hooks, payoff timing, loops that make viewers rewatch, and endings that push to the next video, a follow, or comments.
- How to create curiosity: open questions, visual teasers, "wait for it," contrarian claims, stakes, incomplete information.
- Break down at least 15 high-performing videos per brand second by second, labeling each hook, loop and payoff (see "Watching videos directly" below).

### B. Audience types and topic selection
For tricking, compare and give examples of each:
- **General-audience** content (e.g. "how to do a backflip")
- **Specialist** content (e.g. "why your corkscrew looks ugly")
- **Informational/curiosity** content (e.g. "why short people tend to be better at tricking")

For each: typical reach, follower conversion, comment and share behavior, and the role it plays in growing an account (top of funnel vs community vs authority). Recommend the mix.

For taita.talks, evaluate niches such as:
- Business/marketing breakdowns ("top brands that do marketing better than you")
- Life-philosophy/relationship takes ("how your commitment to your partner affects everything")
- Day-in-the-life / student-founder vlogs
- Build-in-public / founder journey
- Plus any other niches research shows fit a 19-year-old Berkeley CS student and founder who also does acrobatics

For each niche: saturation, monetization potential, fit with raw one-take talking videos, and examples of creators winning with it.

### C. Formats and rotation
- Catalogue the formats that work in each niche: tutorials, skits, POV, reaction, stitches/duets, breakdowns, myth-busting, progression/challenge series, "things nobody tells you," storytimes, slideshows, green-screen commentary, etc.
- If he runs several formats per account (e.g. tricking: his own athletic pain points, then tutorials, then skits), how should he rotate them? Do successful creators stick to one format or cycle? How do series and recurring formats build habit and following?
- Give a recommended **weekly format rotation for each account**, assuming high-volume posting and minimal editing.

### D. Captions, on-screen text and packaging
- What on-screen hook text, captions and descriptions work in each niche, with real examples.
- Caption length, CTAs, comment-bait, keyword/SEO captions (TikTok search), hashtags (do they still matter?), and thumbnails/covers for Shorts.
- Japanese-specific caption and text conventions for Gohantomo.

### E. Converting views into followers and customers
- Why accounts like taita.del get huge views but few followers, and the specific fixes: series, profile optimization, pinned videos, identity/personality, recurring characters, follow-worthy promises.
- How creators turn the audience into revenue: coaching, courses, apps, brand deals, communities. Who does it well in tricking, acrobatics, calisthenics, martial arts and parkour?

### F. Gohantomo / Japan
- What diet, calorie and health app marketing works on Japanese TikTok and Instagram. Look at Japanese competitors (あすけん, カロミル, etc.) and any Japanese accounts promoting food-tracking or diet apps organically.
- Do US-style app slideshows (Cal AI, Umax, etc.) work in Japan? Find Japanese examples of the slideshow format and how it's adapted.
- Mascot-led brand accounts in Japan, and how they create content (もぐもぐ could be the face).
- Japanese trends in diet content: ダイエット, 爆食, 食べて痩せる, レコーディングダイエット, コンビニ飯, etc.
- A launch content plan: first 30 posts and their formats.

## Breadth requirement: don't just study viral videos

Study the people and brands that do organic marketing well, not just one-off viral hits. Specifically:

- **Tricking/action sports:** top trickers and tricking brands (e.g. Loopkicks, Tricking Mastery, Tricks Tutorials, Tricking Bible, well-known athletes with big followings), plus adjacent niches that have solved the same problem: calisthenics, parkour/freerunning (Storror and others), gymnastics, breaking, skateboarding, martial arts, and athletic brands (Red Bull, Nike, Gymshark athlete content). Include Japanese trickers and acrobats if relevant.
- **Talking-head / personal brand:** creators who grew with low-production talking videos (e.g. Alex Hormozi short-form, Ali Abdaal, Codie Sanchez, Gary Vee clips, student/founder creators, "day in the life of a CS student" creators), plus marketing-breakdown creators and life/relationship commentary creators. Find young founder-creators especially.
- **App marketing:** how Cal AI, Umax, Rizz, and other consumer apps grew with organic TikTok (slideshows, UGC, faceless accounts, founder-led content), and their Japanese equivalents.

For each studied creator or brand, record: account, platform, approximate following, what formats they use, the hook patterns they repeat, posting cadence, how they monetize, and the specific lesson for Taita.

## Sources and methods

- Use web search and page fetching heavily: creator interviews and podcasts (transcripts), YouTube videos and their transcripts/metadata, articles breaking down viral videos, marketing teardowns, Reddit (r/Tricking, r/NewTubers, r/TikTokCreators, app-marketing communities), X threads from growth marketers, Japanese sources (note.com, Japanese marketing blogs, Japanese TikTok/Instagram trend reports).
- If network access blocks something, note it and move on; don't spend more than a couple of attempts on any one blocked source.
- **Never invent view counts, quotes, or examples.** If a figure is unverified, label it "unverified" or leave it out. Cite a link for every concrete example.

### Watching videos directly
- Install `yt-dlp`, `ffmpeg`, and `faster-whisper` (CPU, `base` or `small` model).
- If yt-dlp or the Whisper model download hits SSL/certificate errors, export `SSL_CERT_FILE=/root/.ccr/ca-bundle.crt` and `REQUESTS_CA_BUNDLE=/root/.ccr/ca-bundle.crt`. Never disable TLS verification.
- For each key example video (aim for 15+ per brand), download it, then:
  1. Get the transcript: use the platform's captions if `yt-dlp` can fetch them, otherwise transcribe with Whisper (use the Japanese language setting for Japanese videos).
  2. Extract frames with ffmpeg: one every 0.5s for the first 3 seconds (the hook), then one every 2s.
  3. View the frames and write a timestamped breakdown merging visuals, on-screen text, and speech: hook, loops opened, re-hooks, payoff, ending/CTA, cut frequency, framing, text placement.
- Delete downloaded media after analysis; commit only transcripts and breakdowns, never the video files.
- Expect YouTube Shorts to work best; TikTok may block cloud IPs; don't attempt anything requiring a login or cookies. If a platform blocks downloads (e.g. "confirm you're not a bot") after 2 attempts, stop trying that platform and use secondary sources (articles, embeds, search snippets, creator interviews) instead. Note which platforms worked in ASSUMPTIONS.md.

  4. Formats, rotation and series design

- Commit and push progress as you go (after each workstream finishes), so nothing is lost if the session stops.
