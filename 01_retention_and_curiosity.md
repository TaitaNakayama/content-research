# 01 — Retention mechanics and curiosity: the hook / loop / payoff playbook

Written for Taita. Evidence labels: **[source]** = a cited source claims it; **[inference]** = my reasoning; **(unverified)** = couldn't confirm. Read this first: **the public evidence on hooks is thinner than the internet makes it sound.** Almost every hard number you see ("80% hold at 3 seconds", "45% of value in 3 seconds") traces to SEO blogs with no dataset. I list what is solid, what is not, and where the video breakdowns (appended below) must settle the rest.

## 0. What is actually solid

| Claim | Source | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Instagram ranks on **watch time, likes per reach, sends per reach**; likes matter slightly more for followers, sends slightly more for non-followers. | Mosseri, Jan 2025, reported by [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-2025/738034/) | Primary (Mosseri quote). Weights are not published. |
| Instagram weighs both % of video watched and total seconds. | Mosseri, via [onlinemarketing.de](https://onlinemarketing.de/social-media-marketing/instagram-so-wichtig-sind-watched-seconds) | Secondary |
| I found **no Mosseri statement** that "the first 3 seconds" decide Reels. Instagram's "View Rate" insight is the share who keep watching after 3s. | Search results above | The "3-second rule" is a convention, not a documented ranking rule. |
| YouTube Shorts: Paddy Galloway studied 3.3B views / 33 channels / 5,400 Shorts. Shorts with **viewed-vs-swiped-away (VVSA) under 60% rarely performed well**; best performers were mostly **70-90%**; high VVSA did not guarantee success; "treat the intro like a thumbnail"; likes/shares/comments showed **no strong relationship** with performance. He labels this preliminary. | [Galloway thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1646898356419981315.html) | Best primary evidence in this file. Dates from 2023; YouTube has changed Shorts view counting since. |
| Same study: most Shorts were 20-40s; Shorts with average view duration above 50s averaged 4.1M views (confounded). | same | Primary, correlational |
| YouTube Shorts counts a view on start or replay since 31 Mar 2025; **engaged views exclude loops**. | [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/double-youtube-watch-time-looping-shorts/) | Secondary, consistent with glossaries |
| TikTok's ad-side analysis: most recall impact falls in the first 6 seconds; showing a person in the first couple of seconds raised "hooking power" 50%; creator saying "you" in the first 5s raised purchase intent 128%. | [Marketing Brew on TikTok x CreatorIQ report](https://marketingbrew.com/stories/2023/12/18/pay-attention-to-me-report-shares-brand-tips-for-catching-users-interest-on-tiktok) | Primary-ish but **ads, not organic**. |

What I could not find: TikTok's published ranking weights, any controlled test of "follow for part 2", any creator data splitting scroll-stop (first frame) from listen-decision (first words).

## 1. Hook archetypes (first 1-3 seconds)

No public retention data compares archetypes against each other. What follows is a catalogue of what creators actually use, with what each does mechanically **[inference]**. Examples are from titles/snippets I could reach; the second-by-second pass will confirm the spoken opening.

| Archetype | Mechanism [inference] | Fits | Real example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Contrarian claim** | Violates a belief the viewer holds; resolving it requires watching | Authority, reach | Codie Sanchez, ["Money doesn't care how you made it"](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/g1VGWLhDC2M); ["Why she treats her businesses like bonds, not growth stocks"](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/krBG8-R0jFU) |
| **Open question** | Viewer silently answers; gap opens | Reach, mindset | Codie Sanchez on starting a business without capital opens by asking whether you need money, then "think again" (per search snippet) [link](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/2OITHPOWG5M); Dr Julie, ["Am I Depressed?"](https://www.youtube.com/embed/WDiLJJ753ww) (~4.4M views per snippet) |
| **Negation / "stop doing X"** | Implies the viewer is currently doing X; loss-aversion | Reach, community | Gary Vaynerchuk, ["Stop blaming everyone else"](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/LSuV1SHcd8Q); Sahil Bloom, ["Stop Saying Later"](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/XcfqhnHr2fg); Dr Julie, ["You're Not Lazy - 3 Reasons You're Procrastinating"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1RUdv6UFXQE) |
| **Incomplete information** | Withholds the key noun | Authority | Dr Julie, ["What Nobody Tells You About Grief and Loss"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UoUDhJ1DCXg); Sahil Bloom, ["This One Sentence Rewired..."](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/UdqvX_FO6Bk) |
| **Stakes declaration / direct address** | "You" plus a situation raises personal relevance. TikTok's ad data supports saying "you" early [source, ads only] | Community, conversion | Mel Robbins, ["You need to hear this"](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/3O40KZfNTbE), ["If You Are Struggling To Make A Decision"](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/njZtqUek6mU) |
| **Credential flash** | Borrowed authority in under a second | Authority | Gary Vee short titled with "Built $500M VaynerMedia" (title only; whether spoken is unverified) |
| **Visual teaser / in-media-res** | The frame itself shows the outcome or mid-action | Tricking, anything with a visual | Your own crash clip is this by default. Needs visuals, so brand 1 only. Jenny Hoyos describes a hook that is clear on mute and foreshadows the payoff [per secondary interview summaries](https://videohighlight.com/v/As7abwNhG7Y) |
| **"Wait for it"** | Promises a late payoff explicitly | Reach | I found no verified example from a top creator. Treat as weak. [inference] it spends a hook-second on a promise instead of content |

Practical read **[inference]**: the archetypes top talking-head creators repeat are **negation, contrarian claim, open question and incomplete information**, all of which need only words. A viewer who finishes the hook and cannot tell what the video is about is the failure case.

## 2. First frame vs first words

- Jenny Hoyos treats the first frame as the key hook and wants it understandable on mute [secondary summaries](https://videohighlight.com/v/As7abwNhG7Y). Galloway says treat the intro like a thumbnail [source](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1646898356419981315.html). TikTok's data says a visible person within the first couple of seconds helps [source, ads](https://marketingbrew.com/stories/2023/12/18/pay-attention-to-me-report-shares-brand-tips-for-catching-users-interest-on-tiktok).
- Sound-off share (68% TikTok, 54% Reels) is circulating, but I could not trace it. **(unverified)**
- **[inference] two decisions:** (1) scroll-stop on the frame and any text, which happens before audio matters; (2) listen-decision in roughly the first sentence. A talking video has one frame, your face, so the controllable variables are expression, framing, mid-gesture start (don't begin with "hey guys"), and an on-screen title typed in the app.

## 3. Mid-video re-hooks

No retention-curve data published on re-hooks that I could verify. Techniques creators use **[inference from observed formats]**:

| Re-hook | How it looks in words | One-take safe? |
|---|---|---|
| Enumeration | "The third one is the problem." Dr Julie's "3 Reasons" format gives built-in checkpoints | Yes |
| Contrast pivot | "But here's the thing..." / but-therefore structure, recommended by Hoyos per [secondary summaries](https://videohighlight.com/v/As7abwNhG7Y) | Yes |
| Question re-ask | "So why does that matter?" | Yes |
| Pattern interrupt (cut, zoom, sound) | Visual/audio change | No (needs editing). Note a CapCut guide distinguishes hook (reason to stay) from interrupt (breaks autopilot) [CapCut](https://www.capcut.com/create/pattern-interrupt-technique-short-form-video) |
| Vocal change | Drop volume, slow down, pause | Yes, free |

## 4. Open loops and payoff timing

- An open loop in a 20-45s video is **one unanswered question the viewer cares about, stated in the first sentence and answered after the body**. Guides advise opening early, closing last, stacking several loops and closing the newest first, and warn that teasing without paying off burns trust [socialync](https://www.socialync.io/blog/open-loops-in-content-2026) (secondary; the Hormozi attribution of Hook-Retain-Reward comes only from derivative notes and I could not confirm it in the book).
- How many: **[inference]** one primary loop plus one small nested one in a 30s video. Three or more risks the viewer losing the thread.
- Payoff placement: no verified study. Galloway's data says holding viewers to the end correlates with performance, and 20-40s was the modal length [source](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1646898356419981315.html). **[inference]** pay off late in the final third, not at the very end, so the last seconds are a closing line rather than dead air.
- Curiosity theory: Loewenstein's information-gap view says curiosity arises from a perceived gap and fades if left unsatisfied too long [summary](https://www.skyword.com/contentstandard/creativity/the-curiosity-gap-how-consumer-psychology-is-driven-by-whats-missing/). Practical consequence: do not hold the loop open longer than the video.

## 5. Rewatch loops

- Case: Jake Fellman's looped 3D Minecraft Shorts; percentage viewed went from 96.8% (Jan 2021) to 187.1% (Apr 2021) and one loop Short went from about 1M to 20M views (self-reported, not independently verified; vidIQ does not claim looping alone caused it) [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/double-youtube-watch-time-looping-shorts/).
- Caveats: replays count as views but **engaged views exclude loops**, and YouTube's feed watch hours don't count toward the 4,000-hour threshold [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/double-youtube-watch-time-looping-shorts/). On Instagram, watch time is a top signal, so rewatches plausibly help [inference]. TikTok weighting of rewatch is undocumented in what I found.
- Types: seamless visual loop (needs matched frames), re-contextualizing ending, detail-hunting. **Only a verbal loop survives one-take**: end on a sentence that grammatically flows into your first words ("...and that's why", then the video starts "most people never ask"). Untested; flag as experiment.

## 6. Endings and CTAs

- **No controlled evidence** that "follow for part 2" outperforms other endings. Series-oriented sources argue a concrete next thing converts better than a bare "follow me", with follow rates of 0.5-3% (no sources given) [socialk.it, secondary](https://socialk.it/en/blog/tiktok-series-content-strategy). Treat as folklore.
- Galloway found likes/shares/comments had no strong relationship with Shorts performance [source](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1646898356419981315.html); Mosseri says sends matter for reaching non-followers [source](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-2025/738034/). So **[inference]**: for reach on Instagram write endings that make someone want to *send* it ("send this to someone who..."); on YouTube optimise retention, not comment bait.
- Strongest evidenced follow mechanism is a recurring format: Kyran Nijjar's daily series opened identically each day and later added cliffhangers ("it programs our minds to come back"), ~2M followers by Nov 2019, ~3M by Apr 2020 [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/?p=145040). Kris Collins brings back recurring characters every week or two [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/why-tiktok-creator-kris-collins-takes-a-scripted-approach-to-content-and-doesnt-rely-on-popular-trends-to-gain-followers). **[inference]** this is the direct answer to your 10M-views / 6k-followers problem: viewers need a named, repeating thing to follow.
- Cost of weak ending: unverified numerically. Qualitatively, a fade-out ("so yeah, that's it") wastes the final second where the loop could restart.

## 7. Curiosity engineering vs clickbait

- Specificity and numbers: Dr Julie's "3 Reasons", "3 Tips" titles; Codie's named "24-Hour Rule". Definite referring expressions ("this one sentence") create a gap with little content, which is how headline research describes clickbait [Kingston study page](https://eprints.kingston.ac.uk/47719).
- Naming an enemy: "Stop blaming everyone else" (Gary Vee). **[inference]** works because it names a behaviour not a person.
- Clickbait that burns retention = a gap the body doesn't close. Faceless.so notes high viewed-rate with low retention means the hook created interest the body didn't fulfil [source](https://faceless.so/blog/youtube-shorts-analytics-7-metrics-to-track). Galloway's VVSA is the diagnostic: high VVSA, poor retention = hook outran content.
- Test: could a viewer who only read your first sentence correctly guess the payoff category? If not, it's a tease, not a promise.

## 8. Decision table

| Purpose | Hook | Loop | Ending |
|---|---|---|---|
| Top-of-funnel reach | Negation or contrarian claim on a common belief | One loop, pay off in last third | Sendable line ("send this to...") or verbal loop back to start |
| Community | Direct address / stakes ("if you're 19 and...") | Light; story arc | Question that only your people can answer, in your voice |
| Authority | Incomplete information or counted list | Enumeration doubles as re-hooks | Name the next topic in the series, not "follow me" |
| Conversion (coaching/app) | Problem-qualifying hook ("if you can't land X") | Loop = the solution | Specific next step stated once (e.g. one line in bio) |

## 9. What applies to a one-take talking video with no editing (taita.talks)

| Technique | Survives? |
|---|---|
| Contrarian / negation / open question / incomplete-info hooks | **Yes** — words only |
| Stakes and direct address | **Yes** |
| Credential flash | Yes, as a spoken clause ("I'm a 19-year-old Berkeley CS student who...") only if relevant |
| Visual teaser, in-media-res B-roll, "wait for it" visuals | **No** |
| Enumeration re-hooks, "but here's the thing", question re-ask, vocal change | **Yes** |
| Cuts, zooms, SFX pattern interrupts | **No** (substitute: move, pause, change volume) |
| Open loop: one question, paid off late | **Yes**, needs only a plan |
| Seamless visual loop | **No** |
| Verbal loop (last sentence feeds first) | **Yes**, experimental |
| Recurring named segment as follow reason | **Yes** — the highest-leverage item for follow conversion [inference] |
| On-screen title | Only if the app's native text tool is acceptable (10 seconds) |

Pre-record checklist (30 seconds, no editing): write the first sentence and last sentence before filming (Hoyos's method per [secondary summaries](https://videohighlight.com/v/As7abwNhG7Y)); start mid-thought; one loop; number your points; end on a line that is either sendable or loops to the first.

### The finding that contradicts the standard advice

Everyone says hook in the first three seconds. **In this sample that does not predict views.**
Spearman rank correlation between views and time-to-first-claim is **+0.07**; between views
and words-in-the-first-3s it is **−0.39** (n=17). Within one creator's own channel, Codie
Sanchez (n=3), the *fastest* opener got the *fewest* views (9.4K against 57K and 65K).

Be careful how far you take that. n=17 is small, and it is confounded by channel size,
upload age and format — the slower-opening videos are mostly older uploads from large
channels. It does **not** show that slow starts are fine. What it does show is that big
videos use many different openers, and that "claim within 3 seconds" is a reasonable default
rather than a law. Treat the pacing numbers above as norms to hit, not as a causal lever.

### Where the hook isn't verbal at all

The tricking corpus produced the most important negative result in this research. The
hook there is frequently **not in the speech**:

- Storror's rooftop POV Short — **97,675,507 verified views** — contains only **106
  recognised words** and has no verbal hook.
- 4 of 18 tricking videos open with no usable words at all (silence or music).
- Xander 73's million-view Shorts are wordless.

So for spectacle content the hook lives in the first *frame* and the *title*. Since titles
are observable, that is where the tricking analysis went:
[`_TITLE_ANALYSIS_del_xander73.md`](video_breakdowns/_TITLE_ANALYSIS_del_xander73.md)
reads all 48 of his Shorts and finds English titles running at a **694,000 median against
204,000** for his Chinese ones.

### Japanese openings

The Japanese Shorts are the fastest openers in the whole corpus: the claim lands at
**0.04–2.4s**, using a quoted viewer question that is then immediately flipped —
「痩せたいので夜ご飯抜いた方がいいですか」 answered with
「そういうことするからいつまで当たっても痩せないのさ」. Long-form Japanese does the
opposite, opening on a greeting and reaching its first claim at 6–26s. The highest-view
Japanese video in the corpus opens on a **verdict** (a BMI judgement) before any story.
