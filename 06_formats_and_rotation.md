# 06 — Formats, Rotation and Series Design

Written for Taita. Scope: format catalogue, rotate-vs-specialise verdict, series mechanics, weekly schedules, batching.

**Read this first: how strong is the evidence?** Public, quantified evidence on "rotate vs specialise" and "do series convert" is thin. Almost everything searchable is creator-tool blog content. I could not reach TikTok or Instagram at all, and YouTube channel pages would not render, so I cannot quote follower or view counts for most accounts. Where I give a number, it is attributed. Where a claim is my reasoning rather than a source's, it is tagged `[inference]`. Items I could not verify are marked `(unverified)`.

---

## 0. The answer in six lines

1. **Specialise on one spine format per account, and rotate only the topic and the "container" inside it.** Do not rotate between unrelated formats.
2. **Your tricking problem is an identity problem, not a reach problem.** A crash clip tells a viewer nothing about why to follow you. A numbered series with a fixed name, cover and opening line does `[inference]`.
3. **Volume you can defend:** tricking 4/wk, talks 3/wk, Gohantomo 5/wk (slideshows cost about 10 minutes each). That is 12 posts/wk across all three accounts, not 21.
4. **Everything is filmed in two sessions per week.** The tricking session is the gym session you already do.
5. **`taita.talks`** uses a single container: one take, one idea, one fixed opening line, no cuts.
6. **Gohantomo** uses slideshows as the volume engine. I found no evidence either way that they work in Japan. Treat the first 3 weeks as a test, not a plan (see 04_gohantomo_japan.md).

## 2. Rotate or specialise? Verdict

**Verdict: specialise on a spine, rotate inside it.** One recognisable promise and one recurring wrapper per account. Vary the topic and the container (tutorial, mistake-fix, progression) within that promise, never the promise itself.

### Evidence for specialising
- **Colin and Samir** (creator-economy interviewers, ~hundreds of Shorts) say of their long-form show: same format every time, same opening question (per a podcast summary at [Creator Science](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/colinandsamir/), secondary). Their rule of thumb for a repeatable format: you can list a dozen ideas in under a minute ([Director's Cut](https://directorscut.kit.com/posts/the-1-reason-creators-burn-out), secondary).
- **Hormozi** (own transcript summary, [Rosetta](https://rosetta.to/u/alexhormozi/how-i-gained-7-8-million-followers-in-40-months-6-key-lessons), secondary): keep a recurring intro and vary it slightly, "warm people like the reminders and cold people need the introduction." He *narrowed* from "relationships and college and food and lifestyle" to business because that audience converted better. In a 90-day test, views rose 2–3x while ad revenue halved, so "all views are not created equal." This matches your 10M-views / 6k-followers problem `[inference]`.
- **Mosseri** (secondary, [Buzz In Content](https://www.buzzincontent.com/news/adam-mosseri-explains-how-creators-can-boost-reach-on-instagram-10610812)): consistency gives audiences "a clear sense of what to expect," which strengthens the reason to follow.
- **Rick Smith Jr.** (a performance creator, so the closest analogue to you): said he "generated a million subscribers off my own Shorts within about a four- or five-month period" after starting to post Shorts "every single day," and was running "30 Shorts in 30 days" ([Tubefilter interview](https://www.tubefilter.com/2022/12/01/youtube-millionaires-rick-smith-jr/amp/), his own words). His Shorts channel is built on a fixed identity: [@RickSmithJr](https://www.youtube.com/@RickSmithJr/shorts). I could not verify the "30 for 30" series itself.
- **Practitioner interviews** (secondary, mixed quality): Planoly's recurring "Sunday Reset" was a creator's best performer ([Planoly](https://planoly.com/blog/30-days-of-tiktok)); a Buffer creator noted funny one-offs went viral but "rarely turned into followers" ([Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/how-i-grew-my-business-on-tiktok/)). That second quote describes your exact problem.

### Evidence for rotating
- One guide proposes ~40% responding to others / 40% original / 20% testing new formats ([InfluenceFlow](https://influenceflow.io/resources/tiktok-content-ideas-the-complete-creators-guide-for-2026)). Unsourced, but it is the only concrete ratio I found.
- Influencer Marketing Hub on Jane August: add formats only when they deepen the core promise ([IMH](https://influencermarketinghub.com/jane-august-tiktok-growth/)).
- Cal AI's own accounts reportedly mix carousels, repurposed faceless videos and original content (secondary, [8x.social](https://www.8x.social/en/blog/cal-ai-multi-account)). Note that is a brand running many accounts, not one creator rotating.

### What happens if you switch abruptly?
- **No official source says a switch is penalised.** YouTube's documentation defines the Shorts metric "Viewed vs swiped away" per Short ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12942217?hl=en&co=YOUTUBE._YTVideoType%3Dshorts)). I found nothing official on channel-level format memory.
- Unsourced SEO guides claim a sudden pivot costs 30–50% of early views and that new-style content may be shown mainly to the subset who liked it ([ttcalculator](https://ttcalculator.net/learn/pivot-tiktok-niche/)). Treat as plausible, unproven.
- `[inference]` Your risk is not an algorithm penalty. It is that a tutorial viewer arriving from a crash-clip audience sees a profile with no obvious promise. The cost of a mixed grid is lost follows, not lost reach.

### Why my verdict is not a shrug
Your goal is followers who buy, and every follow-conversion claim above (Hormozi, Buffer creator, Smith, Mosseri) points the same direction: a repeated promise. The only pro-variety evidence is about *testing* (20%), and nobody sourced says to rotate unrelated formats. So: **80% spine, 20% experiments, and the experiment slot rotates weekly.**

## 3. Series and recurring formats

### What I could and could not establish
- **"Series convert X% better":** a claim of 67% appears on several blogs and is attributed to a 2025 Swydo analysis. I could not find the original. **(unverified; do not rely on it.)**
- **Shorts→subscriber conversion benchmarks** range from 0.05% to 1.9% across blogs, an order of magnitude apart. **(unverified)**. Your own ratio is 6,000 / 10,000,000 = 0.06% of *views*, but views are not unique viewers, so that is a floor, not a rate `[inference]`.
- **"Part 2" being recommended after "Part 1":** one Skool post claims TikTok often serves episode 2 to viewers of episode 1 and suggests pinning a "Part 2" comment ([Skool](https://chat.skool.com/eproductsempire/exactly-how-tiktoks-algorithm-works)). No data given. **(unverified)**
- **TikTok Series** is a separate, paid-paywall feature, up to 80 videos, with eligibility rules from 2023 coverage ([SocialMediaToday](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/TikTok-Launches-Series-Creator-Monetization-Option/644365/)). It is not what I mean by "series" here, and nothing I found says it helps follows.

### Real series that worked (all secondary/commentary except where noted)
- **Ryan Trahan, "50 States in 50 Days"** and the penny series. Playlists exist ([50 states](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLITT7B5zBOm2ywIrnyJptKcILjB6Y_vTy), [penny series](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLPR73zw99dQUMOExTdJa1n1xtJzkHXhtC)). A commentary video asks [how the series "hacked YouTube"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fJP8uGFh4_I) (I did not transcribe it). Colin and Samir devoted an episode to it as a repeatable format. I have no verified follower numbers.
- **Rick Smith Jr.** (above): daily Shorts plus a 30-day run.
- **Numbered diet journeys in Japan:** しゅんしゅんクリニック "【1日目】" and Erina's days "企画③" both number their entries in the title. I do not have performance data.

### Why "Part 7" beats a standalone `[inference]`, with the mechanics
1. A standalone gives a viewer nothing to follow. A numbered entry implies *there is a Part 8*, which is a reason to follow.
2. The number tells a new viewer there is back-catalogue, so they open your profile to start at 1. That is the follow-conversion event you are missing.
3. The viewer who finishes Part 7 is a high-intent warm viewer, which feeds the next video's early retention.
4. Hormozi's point applies: recurring openers serve warm viewers; the first sentence still has to work cold ("assume nothing about new viewers").

### Mechanics checklist (series for you)
| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Name** | Short, searchable, same every time: "Trick of the Day", "Learn Tricking: #12", "Berkeley Founder Log: Day 14", "カロリー1枚診断 #8" |
| **Number** | In the first 2 seconds and the caption. Count up; do not restart. |
| **Cover** | Same layout every time (cover frame with series name + number). For slideshows, slide 1 is the cover. For video, the first frame is the cover, so put the title text where you start. |
| **Opening line** | Same first sentence stem, different noun: "Trick number 12: the cork." |
| **Closing line** | Name the next trick or the next day. This is the "reason to follow." |
| **Profile** | Pin the 3 best series entries; bio states the series name and schedule. |
| **Playlist / collection** | YouTube playlist or TikTok collection per series so entry 1 is discoverable. |
| **Reply with a video** | Answer a comment with the next entry. Costs nothing, produces series-feel. |

### The fix for 10M views / 6k followers `[inference]`
The viral crash and landing clips are one-offs with no series name. Re-upload nothing; instead (a) pin them on profile, (b) make the **next 12 weeks all "Learn Tricking #N"** so that someone arriving from a crash clip lands on a profile whose first screen says what happens next, (c) end every crash/landing clip with "tutorial for this is #N on my page." I could not test this; it is the leading hypothesis, not a proven fix.

## 4. Weekly rotations

**Realism check.** A full-time student who trains and builds a startup should not plan 7 posts/week/account. Count: 21/wk would need about 4 filming sessions plus uploading each time. My defended number is **12/wk total**: 4 tricking, 3 talks, 5 Gohantomo. Gohantomo gets the most because a slideshow needs no filming of you, so it is the cheapest per post. Drop to 3 Gohantomo if Midterm weeks hit.

Times are filming time, not including upload/caption (add 3–5 minutes per post, and I would do uploads in one sitting).

### 4a. taita.del, 4 posts/week

| Day | Format | Purpose | Film time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | **Learn Tricking #N**, single-trick tutorial, one angle, one take | Follow + Auth (the spine) | 15 min |
| Wed | **Mistake-fix** for the same trick family (or a progression update) | Auth, search traffic | 10 min |
| Fri | **Training clip with a payoff**: landing, new trick, or crash. Closes with "tutorial is #N" | Reach (feeds the series) | 5 min (your session footage) |
| Sun | **Experiment slot (the 20%)**: rotate weekly between competition vlog, reaction, Q&A reply, rank journey | Learn what else pulls | 10 min |

Filming: one 60–75 minute block at the gym, Saturday or whenever you already train. No new trips.

### 4b. taita.talks, 3 posts/week (raw one-take)

| Day | Format | Purpose | Film time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tue | **Founder Log: Day N**, what I built / broke this week, one take | Follow (the spine) | 3 min |
| Thu | **One lesson** (hot take or mistake), one idea, under 45 s | Reach | 3 min |
| Sat | **Bridge or storytime**: "what tricking taught me about X" or one personal story | Reach + brand tie | 5 min |

Constraints respected: no cuts, no b-roll, no overlays. Day-in-the-life is deliberately excluded because it needs clips. Film all three in one 20-minute session with a 5-minute re-take allowance. Three posts is what I would defend: with ~100 followers you need reps more than reach, and each take costs 3 minutes, so 5/week is also feasible if the filming habit sticks, but I would not promise it until week 4.

### 4c. ごはんとも, 5 posts/week (after launch)

| Day | Format | Purpose | Film time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | **Slideshow**: "コンビニ◯◯のカロリー" (one food, five slides) | Reach, saves | 10 min |
| Tue | **App demo**: photo → calories, screen recording | Conv | 10 min |
| Thu | **Myth-bust slideshow** (dietitian-style fact; cite a source on the last slide) | Auth, saves | 10 min |
| Fri | **もぐもぐ skit / reaction** (mascot reacts to a food photo) | Follow (running character) | 15 min |
| Sun | **Slideshow experiment**: ranking or "guess the calories" with answer on slide 5 | Learn | 10 min |

Filming: one 60-minute batch session to produce all five. Treat weeks 1–3 as a **test of whether slideshows work in Japan**: the only evidence I found is qualitative (higher saves, per [find-model.jp](https://find-model.jp/insta-lab/tiktok-photo-post/)) and the general engagement edge from Fanpage Karma, which is not Japan-specific. Slideshows cannot be posted to YouTube Shorts as such; reuse the first screen as a video Short only if it costs under 5 minutes.

## 5. Batching under the no-editing constraint

**Two sessions per week, 90 minutes of total filming.**

**Session A: Gym (60–75 min), tricking.**
1. Before you leave: write the 4 trick numbers and their one-line openers on your phone.
2. Film each tutorial **as the first thing you do with fresh legs**: 2 takes max, then move on. Do the three tutorial/mistake clips back to back from the same tripod spot so the framing (and the cover) stay identical.
3. Film the payoff clip and the experiment during your normal training; do not stage them.
4. Name files by the post slot (`MON-learn-12`, `FRI-payoff`) so scheduling takes 2 minutes.

**Session B: Desk (35–45 min), talks and Gohantomo.**
1. Same shirt/background/lighting setup each time so the three talks look like one batch.
2. Say all three talks consecutively, 2 takes each. Do the numbered one first.
3. Gohantomo: shoot all the food photos in one pass (konbini run, 15 minutes), then build 5 slideshows from a fixed template in the posting app. Screen-record the demo in the same sitting.

**Scheduling.** Use each platform's native scheduler (TikTok web scheduling, Instagram, YouTube Studio) so the batch posts itself. I did not verify current scheduler limits, so check them before relying on a 7-day lead.

**Do not batch:** crash/landing clips and anything time-sensitive; they ride on whatever your training produces.

**One rule that protects the constraint:** if a format needs an edit to work, it is out of the rotation. That excludes underdog-reveal skits, compilations and day-in-the-life vlogs for now.
