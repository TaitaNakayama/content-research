# taita.talks: niche evaluation and recommendation

Written for Taita. Data pulled 2026-11-05. Subscriber counts and Shorts view counts are from the YouTube channel and Shorts-tab pages themselves (a snapshot of each channel's most recent Shorts, not lifetime). TikTok and Instagram pages could not be reached, so anything about those platforms is marked unverified. `[inference]` marks my own reasoning.

## TL;DR

1. **Run one pillar: "a Berkeley CS athlete-founder, documented, not taught."** First-person build log of what you are making, what broke, what you decided. Closest proven analog is Marcos Rico Peng: Cal EECS 2022, Cal swimmer, software engineer, startup, 166K YouTube subs, and his Shorts feed already carries paid brand deals.
2. **Run one spice, capped at about 1 in 5 videos: athlete-brain takes** (fear, crashing, discipline, coaching 100+ students) where you are the evidence. This is your actual differentiator and nobody in the business-advice lane has it.
3. **Drop:** marketing breakdowns, relationship/philosophy as a pillar, formal day-in-the-life vlogs, generic AI explainers. Reasons below.
4. **Fix the authority problem by changing the verb**: "I tried / I got wrong / here is what I decided" instead of "you should". Put your age in the hook. Erik Cupsa, Ty Chen and Gohar Khan all do this.
5. **Do not expect Shorts-only reach to carry you.** Alex Hormozi's recent YouTube Shorts sit at 2.1K to 145K views on a 4.61M-sub channel. Plan for one weekly "anchor" talk of 3 to 5 minutes (Edmund Yong's 3 to 4 minute realistic vlogs hit 327K and 366K views) alongside daily raw Shorts.

## 2. Ranked recommendation

1. **Athlete-founder build log (Berkeley CS).** You have an arc nobody else in the lane has: top-8 national tricking, a 10M-view double-back crash, a landing years later, 100+ students coached, now shipping apps (Gohantomo included). Monetization path is concrete: your own apps, sponsors from dev tools (Cupsa-style), and later a coaching or "how to build as a student" product. [inference on the product path.]
2. **Athlete-brain takes, 1 in 5 videos.** "What a double-back crash taught me about shipping." The athlete crossover is the reason a stranger follows *you* instead of Erik Cupsa. Cap it so the feed stays one promise.
3. Bicultural and college content: sprinkle only when it advances the build story.

Drop: marketing breakdowns (authority + produced-channel competition), relationship pillar (authority + no monetization fit), formal DITL (needs editing).

Followers, not views, is your real issue (10M views, ~6K followers on del). Therefore every talks video must state who the account is for in the first 10 seconds: "I'm 19, Berkeley CS, building apps, and I do competitive tricking." [inference]

| Show the failure | Cupsa's quit-startup video; Noel Deyzel's "figured it out alone" origin framing | links in swipe file |
| Borrow credibility from a hard credential | Marcos Rico Peng's Olympic training title; yours is top-8 national tricking | https://www.youtube.com/@marcosricopeng |

Rules for you:
- Never say "you should". Say "I tried", "I got this wrong", "here is what I'd do next time".
- Authority claims only on things you can show: tricking rank, coaching 100+ students, shipping apps.
- Business strategy outside your lived experience is off limits; that is the 40-year-old operators' ground.
- Do not copy Cluely's Roy Lee style of provocation (EO and TechCrunch coverage exists); it relies on controversy that a 100-follower account cannot absorb. [inference]

## 5. Format rotation for raw one-take

You cannot cut, so vary these seven dials. Combine one value from each per video.

| Dial | Values |
|---|---|
| Setting | desk, dorm, gym floor (tricking), walking Sproul/campus, parked car, kitchen, post-training floor |
| Framing | selfie close, propped phone medium, wide with a trick in background |
| Start | cold open on claim; mid-thought ("so, the second I landed that..."); question to camera |
| Length | 15s, 30s, 60s, one 3-5 minute anchor per week |
| Single prop | laptop screen, a failed app screenshot on phone, a pair of grips/shoes, a coffee, your Berkeley ID |
| Motion | static, walk-and-talk, mid-stretch, sitting on floor after a session |
| Language | English; one line of Japanese then translate; full Japanese video for the Gohantomo audience |

Weekly template (no editing): 1 anchor talk (3-5 min, build log), 3 raw Shorts (60s or less, one claim), 1 athlete-brain take, 1 bilingual bit.

## 6. Thirty one-take video ideas

Hooks are spoken unless noted. Items in [brackets] are for you to fill with true facts; I have not asserted any numbers about your life beyond the brief.

**Build log (pillar 1)**
1. "I'm 19, I go to Berkeley, and I'm building a calorie app for Japan. Here's week one."
2. "I just spent [N] hours on a feature nobody will use. Here's the mistake."
3. "Why I'm making a Japanese app instead of copying Cal AI in English."
4. "Nobody at Berkeley tells you the CS classes don't teach you how to ship."
5. "This is the ugliest screen of my app. I'm showing you before the redesign."
6. "I almost quit the app yesterday. Here's the exact moment." (walk-and-talk)
7. "Show-and-tell: I'm going to read you the worst App Store review I could get."
8. "I'll tell you what I'd do differently if I started my first startup today."
9. "Week [N] update: [real metric] users, [real metric] revenue, nothing else." (phone propped on laptop)
10. "How I use AI to write code without skipping the learning. [only if true for you]." (on-screen: laptop)

**Athlete x founder (spice)**
11. "I crashed a double back flip in front of millions of people. Here's what that taught me about launching."
12. "Landing it years later was easier than the first attempt, and the reason isn't skill."
13. "Ranked eighth in the country, still afraid of the same trick."
14. "Tricking taught me to bail early. Startups don't, and that's why I'm learning it the hard way."
15. "I've coached over 100 students. The one mistake they all make is also what founders do."
16. "My training schedule vs. my CS schedule. Something has to give." (gym floor)
17. "Why I film the crash and not the clean run."
18. "Nobody tells you that pain is information. In sport and in code."

**College / student life**
19. "What a 19-year-old at Berkeley actually does on a Tuesday. No b-roll, just me talking." (end-of-day, dorm)
20. "The worst advice I got about getting into Berkeley CS." (only if true)
21. "Berkeley CS is not what TikTok says. Here's what is real." (walk to class)
22. "I turned down [real thing] to build this. Was it worth it?"

**Bilingual / bicultural**
23. "I think in two languages and it changes how I build." (switch mid-sentence)
24. "Things that are normal in Japan and weird at Berkeley."
25. "I am making a Japanese app. Being bilingual is not enough, and here is what I got wrong about the language." [only if true]
26. "Say this in Japanese, then in English, and watch what disappears." (single prop: a phrase on paper)

**Short takes (credible, first-person)**
27. "Stop calling it a side project if you don't ship Fridays." (one claim, 20 seconds)
28. "Hot take from someone who fails on camera for a living: you don't need confidence, you need reps."
29. "The first 100 followers: I'm posting this at 100 so you can watch me do it."
30. "I'm going to be honest about what I earn from this account so far: [real number]." (build-in-public ledger)
