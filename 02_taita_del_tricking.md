## 1. The three content tiers

Definitions used: **General** = anyone can understand and want it ("how to do a backflip"). **Specialist** = only people who already train care ("why your corkscrew looks ugly"). **Informational/curiosity** = a surprising fact about the sport or body that needs no skill ("why short people tend to be better at tricking").

### 1.1 General audience

| Dimension | What the evidence shows |
|---|---|
| Reach | Highest ceiling. 奇軒's "From Being Bullied to Brave" has over 500M views per [CNA](https://www.cna.com.tw/news/ahel/202403090019.aspx); his channel total is 6.53B ([vidIQ 2026-11-04](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/UCvw1LiGdyulhnGksJlGWB6g)); I could not independently confirm the 500M figure (section 2.1). ### 1.4 Recommended mix for taita.del

Assumption: you have reach (10M views) but no conversion. So the mix should *not* chase more reach; it should maximise follow-per-view.

| Tier | Share | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Specialist | 40% | Highest follow intent per view and your real moat (national rank 8th in 2025, 100+ students coached). Also feeds coaching revenue. |
| Informational | 35% | Lets non-trickers learn why you are worth following, and uses the Berkeley/CS/physics angle. Best "second hook" after a crash video. |
| General | 25% | Keep a reach engine, but only in **series form** (e.g. "learning X in N attempts"), never as one-off tutorials or standalone crash clips. |

This split now has partial support rather than none: in the benchmark table in section 3.1, the three channels with the best subscribers-per-view (THENX 1.10%, Chris Heria 1.02%, Sam Sulek 1.21%) are all instructional or same-person-talking, while the viral-spectacle channels sit at 0.2–0.3%. That is a reason to weight specialist and informational heavily. One change to the General row: when you do make a reach video, use 奇軒's shape — 51–60s, a two-person reversal, English title (section 2.1) — because it is the only reach format in tricking with verified mega-scale.

This is still my recommendation [inference], not an evidenced optimum. Treat the first 4 weeks as a test: log followers gained per 1,000 views for each tier and shift the split toward whichever wins. Do not tune on view counts alone.

**The format, from his 48 most recent uploads** (titles, durations and views as of 2026-11-04, [vidIQ video table](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@73_tricking/videos/)). 43 of 48 are Shorts; 5 are long-form.

1. **Length is not "about a minute" — it is 51 to 60 seconds, every time it works.** All eleven Shorts in that set with over 1M views run **0:51–1:00**. Shorts shorter than 0:50 top out around 767K. Nothing in between. The biggest are "Level up 🔥" (1:00, **91.9M**), "Who else likes my outfit?" (0:54, **38.3M**), the same title again (0:57, **13.8M**), "He thought he won" (0:51, **19.1M**).
2. **The hook is a reversal, stated in the title.** The repeated pattern is *someone is underestimated or thinks they have won, then the reveal*: "He thought he won" (19.1M), "Never laugh too early." (4.87M), "Flip the Script." (4.82M), "Plot twist: She is the master" (1.08M), "They laughed at her... until this" (709K), "New Coach vs. Old Coach" (289K), "Who wouldn't be angry?😡😡😡" (1.26M).
3. **The second pattern is a direct question to the viewer** — pure comment bait: "Who else likes my outfit?" (38.3M **and** 13.8M — he reused the identical title), "What is your name?" (2.03M), "Tell me your country." (767K), "Can you do it?" (245K), "Watch till the end!" (350K).
4. **It is wordless.** The story is told in action, with a title and on-screen text, not dialogue. That is why it crosses borders and why >90% of the growth is foreign. [inference, but the English titles on a Taiwanese channel make it near-certain]
5. **He reuses titles and he reuses the location.** Same titles, same gym, same two-person setup (him plus a student, a girl, a rival, an "old coach"). There is always a loser and a winner, so the script writes itself. No travel, no scouting, no new location per video.
6. **His tutorials and his long-form are dead.** "How to do gainer" (0:52): 110,903 views. His five long-form videos in that set: 3,368 / 8,756 / 9,147 / 32,326 / 50,091 views, at 12–16 minutes. A channel with 15.8M subscribers cannot get 10,000 people to watch a 13-minute video. **Short-form subscribers do not transfer to long-form.** [this is the most important caution in the document — see 3.1]
7. **Chinese-titled Shorts are the business layer, not the reach layer.** 「台灣最專業的空翻和Tricking教學在凌空」 (334K), 「來找奇軒學Tricking吧」 (49.8K), 「誰想學，凌空專業極武道！」 (55.3K), 「凌空Tricking教學」 (253K), the classroom tour long-form (9.1K). English skits = millions to tens of millions. Chinese posts = 150K–350K, aimed at Taiwanese people who could physically attend his gym.

**What this means for you, concretely.** The copyable asset is not "make story Shorts". It is: *a two-person reversal, filmed in one place, 51–60 seconds, titled in English with the reversal in the title, no dialogue, and a second local-language track that sells the coaching.* You have the gym, the students and both languages already.

Gymshark's model works because the athlete keeps the audience; Red Bull's does not, for the athlete. So: build the account first, accept product/fee deals, keep hosting rights, and never let the best footage you shoot this year live only on someone else's channel. Do not apply to Gymshark — there is no application, and they scout consistent identity, not view totals. [inference]

### 2.5 The Japan angle — verdict

**Verdict: Japanese-language tricking is a real but small niche. It is a good authority-and-collaboration play and a bad reach play. Do not make it your primary language. Use 奇軒's two-track model instead: English-first for reach, Japanese as a second track.**

Evidence for a thin competitive field (this part is strong):
- **Feedspot's 53-channel global tricking directory contains no Japan-based channel.** The only Japan-linked entry is **ZEN-Tricking** at ~6.2K subscribers, whose description mentions the Japan Tricking Association ([Feedspot](https://videos.feedspot.com/tricking_youtube_channels/)). The article **gives no participation or membership figures at all**, and carries 2019 maintenance tags for missing sources ([ja.wikipedia](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%83%88%E3%83%AA%E3%83%83%E3%82%AD%E3%83%B3%E3%82%B0)).
- The Japan Tricking Association is a general incorporated association in Kobe, representative director Taichi Okada. Its own press release gives **no membership number**; the only size figure is that **Hooked2017 — a world event — drew about 800 participants, roughly 1,000 including spectators** ([@Press](https://www.atpress.ne.jp/news/147547)). Evidence **against** making Japanese your reach language (this is the part that changes the recommendation):
- 奇軒 is the only tricking creator at mega scale, and his own data separates the two languages cleanly. **English-titled, wordless Shorts: 1M to 91.9M views. Chinese-titled Shorts: 150K–350K, mostly advertising his own gym.** ([vidIQ video table](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@73_tricking/videos/)) He is a Mandarin-speaking creator in a Mandarin market who built a 15.8M audience by **not** posting in his own language, and both CNA and Business Weekly say the result was >90% overseas subscribers and English comments.

**So the opening is real but it is a different opening than the first pass guessed.** Japanese-language tricking content has almost no competition — but almost no competition in a niche this small is a *credibility* asset, not a growth asset. Do not use it for: your main posting language.

**Cheap test, unchanged:** post 5 videos with Japanese captions on top of English titles and read the country split in analytics. Cost is zero because captions are free. But judge it on *comments and DMs from Japanese trickers*, not on views.

### 3.2 Why your crash specifically did not convert

What is evidenced vs inferred:

**Evidenced (practitioner reports, low rigor):** viewers often follow for one piece of content, not the account; a profile unrelated to the viral post lowers conversion; conversion comes more from consistent mid-performing posts than from one hit; typical view-to-follow rates are tiny ([Toptal](https://www.toptal.com/creator/post/what-happens-when-you-go-viral), [Marketing Right Side Up](https://marketingrightsideup.beehiiv.com/p/dont-want-go-viral-mktrsu-012), [Reddit mirror](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1qqt793/snapshots/2026-01-31T04%3A50%3A42.947805Z)). Your own early viral post, the failed-attempts compilation, was reported as "inspiring others" ([Midpen Post](https://midpenpost.org/2024/05/17/flipping-in-finesse-gunn-juniors-mindset-grows-through-tricking/)), but no follower outcome was reported.

**Inference specific to tricking:**
1. **The audience came for the fall, not for you.** A crash is a self-contained payoff. After the fall, the viewer has no question left that your account answers.
2. **Spectators, not practitioners.** Most views on a crash are from non-trickers who will never follow a tricking account. Practitioners follow; spectators swipe.
3. **No promise on the profile.** A viewer who taps through to your profile sees whatever you posted last. If it is a random mix, "what do I get if I follow?" has no answer.
4. **The comeback was a separate post.** The landing "years later" paid off the crash, but a viewer of one never sees the other unless they connect. An arc only converts if both halves sit in one visible series.
5. **Followers need a repeat reason.** 奇軒 gives one: each Short is a new episode of the same story shape. Storror gives one: Monday 5pm, same faces ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2019/01/16/creators-going-pro-storror/)).

**Comparable cases:**
- Converted: 奇軒 (serial stories, under 10K → 1M in four months from Shorts, [CNA](https://www.cna.com.tw/news/ahel/202403090019.aspx)); Storror (weekly slot, storytelling, merch, though it took ~7 years to reach 1M).
- Did not convert / unclear: Jason Paul's 158M views accrued on Red Bull's channel ([Red Bull](https://www.redbull.com/int-en/jason-paul-best-freerunning-moments)). I found **no documented case** of a crash-viral tricking or parkour athlete who did/didn't convert with numbers. I am not going to invent one. Also unconverted: **breaking**, which reached the Olympics and still has no individual creator audience I could verify — the audience sits on [Red Bull BC One](https://www.youtube.com/@redbullbcone) (1.22M). A judged movement sport does not hand its athletes an audience. [inference]

**Checks you can do in 10 minutes (analytics, not guessing):**
- On each platform: share of views from non-followers; profile visits per 1,000 views; follows per 1,000 views for the crash vs your average video.
- Which country the views came from (Japan vs US vs other).
- **Confirm the platform.** Press says the viral post was an Instagram failed-attempts compilation ([Midpen Post](https://midpenpost.org/2024/05/17/flipping-in-finesse-gunn-juniors-mindset-grows-through-tricking/)). If the 10M sits on Instagram, Instagram is where the fix list runs first, and `taita.del` there is the profile to rewrite.
- Whether the crash views were mostly "FYP/recommended" vs "following."
- Whether profile bio/pinned posts say what you post.

**Fix list (all zero-edit):**
1. Pin the crash and the landing as a visible pair; add a bio line like "Failed it in Kobe. Landed it years later. Learning triple cork next."
2. Turn "the road to X" into a numbered recurring series (section 5).
3. End videos with an open question you answer in the next one ("next: why I keep landing on my left foot").
4. Reply to the best comments from the crash video with new videos.

## 4. Format rotation (high volume, minimal editing)

Assumption: you film 1 to 2 training sessions per week and post about 10 to 14 pieces a week, same file cross-posted to TikTok, Reels and Shorts. No b-roll, no motion graphics; one trim and on-screen text (apps like CapCut auto-caption).

| # | Format | Tier | Edit load | Per week | What it builds |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | **Attempt log**: raw clip of an attempt (fall or land) with one on-screen line "Triple cork, attempt 41" | General (series) | Trim only | 3-4 | Followable arc, episode count |
| B | **Fix-it**: you talk to camera in the gym, 20-40s, "why your X looks ugly", demo once | Specialist | One take | 3 | Authority, coaching leads |
| C | **Curiosity one-take**: sitting in the gym or dorm, one surprising claim and a 30s explanation | Informational | One take | 2-3 | Reach plus identity (Berkeley/CS) |
| D | **Student clip**: your student's before/after (with their permission), you comment | Specialist | Trim | 1-2 | Proof of coaching |
| E | **Rate/react**: stitch or duet a tricking clip, say what is good/bad | Specialist | Platform-native | 1-2 | Community |
| F | **Japan bilingual**: English title, Japanese caption added (never Japanese-only — section 2.5) | Any | Caption only | 1-2 | Japan experiment, authority |

Why high volume: the sources agree that conversion comes from many mid-performing posts more than one hit ([Reddit mirror](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1qqt793/snapshots/2026-01-31T04%3A50%3A42.947805Z)), and the volume numbers back it — 奇軒 815 videos behind 15.8M subs, Hybrid Calisthenics 844 behind 4.47M, Jujimufu 1,470 behind 1.58M, Sam Sulek **1,331** behind 4.57M, Ginger Ninja Trickster 568 behind 1.42M (all vidIQ, 2026-11-04/05).

**Two format rules taken from 奇軒's per-video data (section 2.1), which override anything above if they conflict:** (a) **cut to 51–60 seconds, not 30** — every one of his 1M+ Shorts sits in that band and his sub-50-second Shorts cap out near 770K; (b) **put the reversal in the title in English** ("He thought he won", "Never laugh too early."). Formats A, D and E below take this directly: film with a student, let them win or lose, title it as a reversal.

Monetization route [inference, modelled on Heria Pro and 奇軒's classes]: free fix-it videos → "send me your clip" form check (paid) or a progression program → later an app/course. Do not add the sales step until follows per 1,000 views is above your current baseline.
